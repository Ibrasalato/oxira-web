E-commerce in Saudi Arabia is an active market, and consumers are used to buying from their phones and paying with mada and Apple Pay. But opening a successful store takes more than a nice design: there are regulatory steps, technical decisions, and a plan for attracting customers.

This guide walks through the steps in order. It is specific to Saudi Arabia, and the regulatory requirements may change, so always check them with the official sources.

## Step 1: Decide what you'll sell and to whom

Before anything technical, answer these questions:

- What are your products, and roughly how many are there?
- Who is your target customer, and in which cities?
- How will you deliver orders: a shipping company, your own driver, or pickup from your branch?
- What is your profit margin after shipping costs and online payment fees?

These questions determine the kind of store you need and the right level of investment.

## Step 2: The regulatory framework

### Commercial registration or freelance certificate

To sell online in the Kingdom, your business needs a regulatory framework: a commercial registration (CR), or a freelance certificate for the activities that individuals are permitted to carry out.

### Verifying your online store

Online stores are verified through the **Business Platform** of the Ministry of Commerce, after the service moved there from the "Maroof" platform in 2023. Verification gives your customers extra confidence, and it is a basic requirement for selling through many platforms.

### Disclosures and policies

Show clearly in your store: the business name and contact details, the exchange and returns policy, delivery times, and prices including VAT if you are registered for it. Clarity here protects you and reduces disputes with customers.

### E-invoicing

If you are registered for VAT, you are required to issue electronic invoices according to the requirements of the Zakat, Tax and Customs Authority (ZATCA), known as "Fatoora". Make sure the store or accounting system you use supports this.

> This is a general overview, not regulatory advice. Check the current requirements with the Ministry of Commerce and the Zakat, Tax and Customs Authority.

## Step 3: Ready-made platform or custom store?

| | Ready-made platform (such as Salla, Zid and Shopify) | Custom store |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Speed to launch | Fast | Takes time to develop |
| Upfront cost | Lower | Higher |
| Customisation | Within the platform's limits | Built around your needs |
| Integration with your systems | Through available apps | Built around your systems |

**Start with a ready-made platform** if you're testing the market or have few products. **Consider a custom store** if you have special requirements: wholesale pricing, integration with an in-house inventory or accounting system, a different buying experience, or a sales volume that makes platform fees a burden.

## Step 4: Payments and shipping

- **Payment gateway**: choose a gateway licensed in the Kingdom that supports the methods your customers use, such as mada, Apple Pay and credit cards, and compare fees and payout times.
- **Shipping**: compare shipping companies on coverage, price and cash on delivery, and set a clear policy for shipping fees.
- **Returns**: have a practical way to handle returns ready before your first order.

## Step 5: Product pages that sell

- Clear photos from more than one angle.
- A description that answers customers' questions: size, material, use, delivery time.
- Price and stock availability shown clearly.
- A title and description for every product page to help it show up on Google.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I open an online store without a commercial registration?

In some activities, individuals can sell with a freelance certificate instead of a commercial registration. Check the permitted activities and the current requirements with the relevant authorities.

### Should I start with a custom store?

Not necessarily. Many stores start on a ready-made platform, then move to a custom store when their requirements grow.

### When do I need e-invoicing?

If you are registered for VAT, you are required to use it according to the requirements of the Zakat, Tax and Customs Authority.

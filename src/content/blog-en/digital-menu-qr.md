Customers pointing their phone camera at a QR code on the table has become a familiar sight in many restaurants and cafés. A digital menu is no longer just a substitute for a paper menu; it's a practical way to update prices and items quickly and give your customers a clearer experience.

In this guide we explain what a QR digital menu is, its benefits, what it should contain, tips for printing and placing the code, and how to keep your prices up to date.

## What is a QR digital menu?

It's a food and drinks menu in the form of a web page, which customers reach by scanning a QR code with their phone camera. They don't need to download an app: the page opens straight in the browser, showing items grouped by category with their prices.

## Why do restaurant and café owners use digital menus?

### Update prices without reprinting

An ingredient got more expensive? You added a seasonal drink? An item has sold out? With a paper menu, that means reprinting or a pen correction that looks unprofessional. With a digital menu, you make the change from your account and customers see the update straight away, while the code printed on the table stays the same.

### A clearer experience for customers

- Easy browsing by category: hot drinks, cold drinks, desserts, main dishes.
- Clear text that can be zoomed in on a phone.
- No need to wait for a waiter to bring the menu.

### Hygienic and practical

Paper menus pass through many hands and wear out over time. A code printed on a table card is easier to clean and replace.

### Use beyond the restaurant

You can put your menu link on your social media accounts, on your Google Maps profile, and in your WhatsApp auto-reply. That way customers can see your items and prices before deciding to visit.

## Paper menu vs. QR digital menu

| Criterion | Paper menu | QR digital menu |
|---|---|---|
| Changing prices | Requires reprinting | Directly from your account |
| Adding a new item | Reprint or insert | Instant |
| Sharing online | Needs photographing | Ready-made link |
| Wear and tear | Likely | Only the code needs replacing if damaged |
| Requires the customer's phone | No | Yes |

An important note: some customers prefer a paper menu, such as older people or those without a phone. That's why many restaurant owners keep a few paper copies for anyone who asks.

## What should a digital menu include?

### The essentials

- **Categories**: divide the menu into logical sections that are easy to move between.
- **The name of each item**: clear and understandable; avoid vague names without explanation.
- **Price**: for every item, clearly shown.
- **A short description**: a line or two about the ingredients or preparation where needed.

### Useful information

- **Sizes**: if an item comes in more than one size, show the price of each.
- **Allergens**: such as nuts, milk or gluten, where possible.
- **Calories**: check the regulatory requirements that apply to your business in Saudi Arabia regarding displaying calories and other information, and comply with them.
- **Unavailable items**: hide or update them, rather than letting a customer order one and then apologising.

### Content mistakes to avoid

- A very long list in one category with no subdivision.
- Prices that don't match your point-of-sale system.
- Exaggerated descriptions that don't match the actual dish.

## Tips for printing and placing your QR code

A good code is one that scans on the first try. Here are the most important tips:

1. **The right size**: don't make the code too small. Customers should be able to scan it easily while seated.
2. **Contrast**: a dark code on a light background. Avoid faded colours or busy backgrounds behind the code.
3. **Empty space around the code**: leave a white margin around it and don't place text or graphics right up against it.
4. **Explanatory text**: write a phrase next to the code such as "Scan the code to view the menu".
5. **Test it before the final print**: scan the code with several phones and in the restaurant's actual lighting.
6. **The right material**: use card stock or a laminated card that can withstand frequent cleaning.
7. **A fixed, visible location**: on every table, at the till, and perhaps on the shop door.

## How to keep menu prices up to date

- **Assign a responsible person**: one person who knows they are responsible for updating the menu whenever something changes.
- **Review the menu regularly**: once a week, for example, compare the prices with your point-of-sale system.
- **Update as soon as something changes**: don't put off updating a price or hiding a sold-out item.
- **Choose a system that is easy to edit**: the easier editing is, the more likely the menu stays up to date.

## How to encourage customers to use the digital menu

Some customers aren't used to scanning codes yet, and some hesitate at first. These simple steps help:

- **Have staff point it out**: a short line when customers sit down, such as "The menu is on the code on your table", is enough.
- **Make the page fast**: light images and organised content, so customers don't wait long if the internet is weak inside the venue.
- **Provide Wi-Fi if possible**: and write the network name on the same card if it's available to customers.
- **Consider language**: if some of your customers don't speak Arabic, think about providing item names in a second language.
- **Listen to feedback**: if more than one customer complains that an item or category is hard to read, change it.

No. The customer scans the code with their phone camera, and the menu page opens straight in the browser.

No. The code points to the menu page itself, and any change you make from your account appears on the page without changing the printed code.

### Can I share the menu outside the restaurant?

Yes. You can put the menu link on your social media accounts or your Google Maps profile, or send it to customers on WhatsApp.

That's your decision. Many restaurants rely mainly on the digital menu while keeping a few paper copies for those who prefer them or don't have a phone.

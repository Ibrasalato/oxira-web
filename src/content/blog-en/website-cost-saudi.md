One of the questions business owners type into Google most often is: "How much does it cost to design a website?" The honest answer is that there's no single figure that suits everyone. A brochure website of a few pages is very different from an online store with hundreds of products, a payment gateway and integration with shipping companies.

In this guide we explain the factors that push the cost up or down, the options available to you in the Saudi market, and how to ask for a clear quote that won't surprise you with extra costs later.

## What determines the cost of a website?

### The type of website

The starting point is the question: what do you want from the website?

- **Brochure website**: presents your business, your services and how to contact you. Usually the simplest and least expensive.
- **Service website with booking or ordering**: adds features such as appointment booking or a quote request form.
- **Online store**: needs a shopping cart, a payment gateway, product and stock management, and returns and shipping policies. This type is usually the most expensive because it is more complex to build and run.
- **Platform or custom system**: such as a customer portal or an internal system. These are software projects whose cost varies with the requirements.

### Number of pages and amount of content

Every extra page means time spent on design, writing and review. A five-page website (home, about us, services, portfolio, contact us) is different from a website with a separate page for every service and every branch. And don't forget that writing the content itself can be a separate line item if you don't prepare it yourself.

### Number of languages

Many businesses in Saudi Arabia need a website in Arabic and English. A second language isn't just a matter of translating text; it includes the page direction (right-to-left and left-to-right), reviewing the design in both, and search engine optimisation for each language.

### Custom design or ready-made template?

- **Ready-made template**: faster and less expensive, and suitable for most small and medium-sized projects, as long as it is flexible and lets you change colours, fonts and content.
- **Custom design**: starts from scratch based on your brand identity, and goes through stages such as interface design and then development. It gives you more distinctiveness, but takes longer and costs more.

### Online store and integrations

If your website sells, there are extra items such as connecting a payment gateway, setting up shipping methods, and invoicing. Some of these services have their own fees from their providers, separate from the design cost.

### Hosting, domain and annual renewal

This item is one many people overlook when comparing. A website isn't paid for only once; it has ongoing costs:

- **Domain name**: such as yourbusiness.sa or .com, renewed every year.
- **Hosting**: the server your website runs on, renewed yearly or monthly.
- **SSL certificate**: needed for the padlock to appear in the browser, and sometimes included with hosting.

Always ask: how much will I pay in the second year?

### Maintenance and updates

After launch, you'll need to change prices, add a service or update photos. Can you do that yourself? Or will you pay for every change? This question affects the real long-term cost more than many people expect.

## What options do you have?

### Freelancer

This can be a good option if you find a reliable freelancer with a clear track record. Prices among freelancers vary enormously depending on experience and the type of work. Pay attention to points such as: who owns the hosting and domain accounts? And who will fix any problem after delivery?

### Agency or design company

Usually offers a full team: design, development, content writing and search engine optimisation. Suitable for larger projects or those that need a fully custom design. Prices here also vary a lot from one company to another, so you should compare the details of each quote, not just the total.

### Website builder platform

Gives you ready-made templates you can customise, lets you edit the content yourself, and usually has clear, published pricing. Suitable for anyone who wants a professional website quickly without getting into a long software project.

## How to request a quote that won't surprise you later

Whether you work with a freelancer, an agency or a platform, ask for an itemised quote, and make sure it answers these questions:

1. How many pages are included? And how much does an extra page cost?
2. Is the website in one language or two?
3. Is the design custom or based on a template?
4. Are hosting and the domain included? For how long?
5. How much is renewal in the second year and after?
6. Who owns the domain and the accounts? (Ideally they should be in your name or your company's name.)
7. Can I edit the content myself?
8. What does support after launch include? And how much do changes cost?
9. Are prices before or after tax?
10. What is the expected delivery time?

With these questions you can compare quotes fairly, because a quote that looks low at first can end up more expensive if changes and renewals are costly.

## Tips to reduce cost without sacrificing quality

- **Start with what you actually need**: a clear website of a few pages is better than a large website that never gets updated.
- **Prepare your content in advance**: logo, photos, service descriptions and contact details. This saves time.
- **Choose a solution you can edit yourself**: it saves you the cost of small, recurring changes.
- **Think about annual costs from the start**: don't compare only the first payment.
- **Add features gradually**: start with the basics, then add booking, a menu or other features when you need them.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is there a fixed price for website design in Saudi Arabia?

No. Prices vary widely depending on the type of website, the number of pages and languages, how it is designed, and who builds it. It's best to request detailed quotes and compare them item by item.

### Is a template-based website enough for my business?

For most small and medium-sized projects, yes. A good template can be customised with your colours, logo and content, and gives you a professional website quickly. A fully custom design makes more sense when you have special requirements or a visual identity that needs a different treatment.

### How long does it take to launch the website?

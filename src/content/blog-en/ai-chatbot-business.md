A customer visits your website at 11 p.m. with a simple question: "Do you offer this service?", "Roughly how much does it cost?" or "What are your opening hours on Friday?" If they can't find the answer quickly, they may close the page and go back to Google to try a competitor.

This is where an AI chatbot comes in: an assistant that answers visitors' questions at any time, based on information about your business. In this article we explain what it is, where it helps, what its limits are, and how to use it responsibly.

## What is an AI chatbot?

A chatbot is a chat window on your website where visitors type a question and get a written reply. There are two main types:

- **Traditional chatbot**: relies on predefined buttons and options, such as "Press 1 for prices". Useful for simple questions, but limited when visitors phrase questions in their own words.
- **AI chatbot**: understands questions written in natural language and composes a suitable reply based on the information available to it, such as the content of your website.

## How can it help your business?

### Replies outside working hours

A chatbot doesn't need a day off or a break. It can answer visitors at night and on holidays, when your team isn't available.

### Fewer repetitive questions

Many questions come up every day: your location, opening hours, available services, how to book. The chatbot answers them and leaves your team the cases that need a human touch.

### Capturing potential customers

When a visitor shows interest and leaves their contact details, those details can reach you so you can follow up at the right time, instead of losing the opportunity.

### Guiding visitors to the right page

Instead of making visitors search the site themselves, the chatbot can point them to the right service, booking or contact page.

## Examples by type of business

- **Clinics**: answering questions about services, opening hours and how to book, without giving diagnoses or medical advice.
- **Salons**: questions about available services and appointments, then directing the customer to the [booking system](/en/products/booking-system/).
- **Restaurants and cafés**: questions about location, opening hours and delivery, with a link to the [digital menu](/en/products/digital-menu-qr/).
- **Service companies**: explaining services and collecting contact requests from interested visitors.

## The limits of a chatbot: what to know before you start

AI is a useful tool, but it isn't a complete employee. It's important to keep your expectations realistic.

### It can make mistakes

AI models can sometimes produce an inaccurate answer or misunderstand a question. So:

- Keep your website information accurate and up to date, because the quality of replies depends on it.
- Review the chatbot's replies and test it yourself with different questions before relying on it.
- Don't rely on it for sensitive decisions, such as medical, legal or financial matters.

### There should be a human behind it

A good chatbot knows when to say "I don't know" and refer the visitor to your team. Complaints, special cases and complex requests need a real person. Make sure visitors can always reach a human contact channel.

### Privacy

- Don't ask visitors for sensitive information through the chatbot.
- Be clear with visitors that they are talking to an automated assistant, not a staff member.
- Handle the contact details visitors leave responsibly, and use them only for the purpose they were given.
- Review the regulations in force in Saudi Arabia on personal data protection, and comply with them.

## Chatbot vs. manual replies

| Criterion | Manual replies only | Chatbot with human follow-up |
|---|---|---|
| Replies outside working hours | Usually unavailable | Available |
| Repetitive questions | Take up the team's time | Answered by the bot |
| Complex cases and complaints | Handled by the team | Referred to the team |
| Accuracy of replies | Depends on the employee | Depends on the accuracy of the website's information, and it can make mistakes |

The idea isn't to replace your team, but to free them for the work that really needs them.

## Questions to ask yourself before adding a chatbot

Not every business needs a chatbot at every stage. Before deciding, consider these questions:

- **Do I get enough visitors?** If your website is new and has few visitors, it may be better to work on showing up on Google first.
- **Do the same questions keep coming up?** If your team answers the same questions every day, a chatbot is a good candidate to lighten that load.
- **Is my website information complete?** The chatbot only knows what is in front of it. If your services or opening hours aren't listed, it can't answer about them correctly.
- **Who will follow up on contact details?** Assign someone to respond to interested visitors who leave their details, so opportunities aren't lost.
- **Which cases should the bot not handle?** Such as complaints or specialist consultations, and make it clear to visitors how to reach your team for them.

If your answers point to repetitive questions, clear information and someone to follow up, a chatbot will most likely be a useful addition.

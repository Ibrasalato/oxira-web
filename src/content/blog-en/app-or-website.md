"I want an app for my business" is one of the requests we hear most often. Sometimes an app really is the right answer; sometimes a good website is faster, cheaper and achieves the same goal. The wrong decision can mean an app that costs a lot and that nobody downloads.

In this article we help you decide with a few simple questions.

## The basic difference

- **A website** can be opened by anyone from a link, with nothing to download, on a phone or a computer. It also appears in Google search results.
- **A mobile app** is downloaded from the App Store or Google Play, stays on the user's home screen, and can send notifications and use the device's features.

## When is a website enough?

A website is usually the better choice when:

- **Most of your customers visit you once or only occasionally**: such as a law firm, a contracting company, or a clinic that patients visit from time to time.
- **You want new customers to find you on Google**: apps don't appear in search results the way websites do.
- **Your goal is to present your services and receive orders or bookings**: this can be done with a website plus an [appointment booking system](/en/products/booking-system/) or an order form.
- **Budget and time are limited**: a website is faster to build and cheaper to maintain.

## When do you need a mobile app?

An app is worth the investment when:

- **Your customers use your service frequently**: daily or weekly orders, account tracking, or a loyalty programme.
- **Notifications are a core part of the service**: order status updates, appointment reminders, or a bus arrival alert.
- **You need device features**: real-time location, the camera, or working offline.
- **You have internal users**: field staff, sales reps or teachers who need a daily work tool.

An example from our own products: in the [Classti school management platform](/en/classti/), students need to know when their bus will arrive, and teachers need to take attendance in seconds. That is frequent daily use that depends on notifications and location, so an app was the right choice. School administrators, on the other hand, work from a web platform on their computers.

## Quick comparison

| | Website | Mobile app |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Access | Directly from a link | Needs a download |
| Visibility on Google | Yes | Limited |
| Notifications | Limited | Full |
| Device features | Limited | Full |
| Cost and time | Lower | Higher |
| Updates | Instant | Go through app store review |

## Questions to help you decide

1. How many times a month will a customer use your service?
2. Do you need to send customers a notification at a specific time?
3. Does the use depend on the user's location or camera?
4. Are most of your customers new people searching on Google, or existing customers who come back?
5. Do you have a budget to maintain and update the app after launch?

If most of your answers point to frequent use and notifications, an app is a logical choice. If they point to new customers and occasional visits, start with a strong website.

## The solution that combines both

Many successful businesses start with a website that brings in customers from Google and presents their services, then add an app later for loyal customers once demand is proven. In that case, the system should be built from the start so that the website and the app share the same data and dashboard, so you aren't running two separate systems.

## Tips before you commission an app

- **Start with a small first version** focused on the core task, then develop it based on real usage.
- **Register the developer accounts in your company's name** on the App Store and Google Play so you keep ownership of the app.
- **Plan for updates**: iOS and Android are updated every year, and an app needs ongoing maintenance.
- **Think about the dashboard**: who will manage the content, users and orders?

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I turn my website into an app later?

An app that uses the same data and dashboard can be built if the system is designed to allow it. Discuss this with the development company from the start.

### Does an app replace a website?

Rarely. A website remains important for visibility on Google and for new customers who won't download an app before they know you.

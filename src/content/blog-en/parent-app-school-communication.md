In many schools, communication with families is spread across a WhatsApp group per class, text messages for absences, phone calls about fees and paper notes sent home with students. The result: parents don't know where to find information, teachers answer messages after hours, and the school never sees the full picture.

A parent app solves much of this, as long as it is part of the school's own system rather than a separate tool.

## What do parents want to see every day?

- **Today's timetable**: the lessons and who teaches them.
- **Attendance**: did my child arrive, and when?
- **Homework and grades**: what is due, and the latest results.
- **Fees and payments**: what is owed, installments, and a way to pay.
- **Dismissal**: who will pick the student up, and confirmation of handover.
- **Messages and announcements**: from the school and the teacher, in one place.

When this information sits in one app and updates automatically from the school system, repeated questions drop sharply.

## Why WhatsApp groups are not enough

- **Privacy**: families' numbers are visible to each other, and student details circulate in open groups.
- **Things get lost**: an important announcement disappears among dozens of messages.
- **No link to data**: a chat group knows nothing about attendance, fees or grades.
- **Pressure on teachers**: answering after hours becomes expected.

WhatsApp can still carry official notices, but from an official business account connected to the system, not from personal numbers.

## Features that make a difference

### Recorded dismissal

Set who may pick up each student and record the handover at the gate. This reassures families and protects the school.

### Canteen wallet

Parents set a spending limit, and items that trigger a child's allergies can be blocked automatically.

### Positive behaviour points

Encouragement parents can see, so communication is not only about problems.

## How to launch the app well

1. **Start with the data**: make sure timetables, attendance and fees are up to date before inviting families.
2. **Invite families with a clear message**: the download link, how to sign in, and why the app is worth their time.
3. **Move communication over gradually**: announce a date after which official announcements are in the app only.
4. **Train teachers**: the teacher app is the source of the attendance, homework and grades parents see.
